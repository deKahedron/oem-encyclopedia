For most of a century since the [[Umaran Empire|Umaran]] capital of [[Nadiria]] fell, the people of [[Fuegil & the Manilands]] have been the front line of the [[Umaran Empire]] against the [[Chalican|chalican]] invaders.

By order of the [[Weylin Damasil|Emperor]], the citizens of the [[Fuegil & the Manilands|Manilands]] were expected to contribute to the defence of their home. The intent was always to support this effort with imports food imports from the Eastern Empire, but the available trade routes were through Nadiria, across 2 foreign nations, or by sea, these supplementary resources never met their promised quotas.

The unity of the [[Fuegil & the Manilands|Manilands]] relied then not on the eastern powers, but on a strong new national identity as survivors of a hundred small populations who had been wronged by a common enemy, old feuds snuffed out by the quest to reclaim their ancestral lands. The [[Sacrum Sophia]], an old but somewhat obscure gnomish religion, became widely adopted by most of these cultures alongside their own traditional gods. This common religion, along with the patchwork language [[Borderspeech]], formed the basis of the new shared culture.

In the wake of the [[The Hunger War|Hunger War]] and the rising [[Demon|demon]] cultism in the north, the [[Sacrum Sophia]] cracks down on its anti-arcane and anti-academic policies in the _[[Carta Ascetica]]_. While previously only certain magical disciplines were banned, this is extended to include not only the practice, but also the study and dissemination of arcane texts.

In Winter of 3432, [[Akôrdia Glamora]] publishes a critique of the [[Sacrum Sophia]]. The crux of her argument is that the banning of any sort of knowledge is antithetical to the entire system of faith.

When rumours began to spread that the [[Glamoran Reformists|Glamoran reformists]] were in talks to ally with [[Zaledrix Almeyar]] of [[Arcaxius]], the Sacrum dispatched a squadron of [[Humble Order]] [[Knight|Knights]] led by [[Laubrey Lafay|Captain Sir Laubrey Lafay]] to learn what they could about the liches and try to sabotage the alliance or else burn the [[Caeran|Library]].

Almeyar revealed to the knights that she had uncovered their subterfuge, presenting the [[Jet-Spun Braid]], a token of the alliance she had formed. Rather than return in defeat, the captain chose martyrdom, sacrificing himself to entrap the liches in an [[Anti-Magic|anti-magic]] field that would prove instrumental in their downfall.

Then [[The Broken Minute|time broke]].

The sudden disappearance of a minute of every day shook every religion across the continent. Reformist tutors declared this a divine punishment for the Sacrum’s heresies against enlightenment. The Sacrum’s official statement was that the destruction of the minute was a side effect of the evil of the liches, and its sacrifice should be commemorated and honoured alongside that of [[Laubrey Lafay|Saint Laubrey the Lichbreaker]] and [[Lena of Haighfeld|Saint Lena the Holy Knife]].

Doomsayers across all Oem took the loss of the minute as the start of some great and terrible apocalyptic countdown. Over the following weeks, many diviners were able to confirm the incident as an isolated one. The Manilands were much less informed of this fact: the Sacrum’s laws outlawed any independent diviners and the few such reformist scholars kept the truth quiet since it didn’t quite suit their narrative.

Since the outbreak of this civil war of faith, [[Enoch Harlaw|Imperial Admiral Enoch Harlaw]] has taken to sea with the Gillish fleet, joining the reformists. Some of his ships have been seen making the crossing south of [[Nadiria]], towards [[Dûnar]].

The borders of the re-conquest have been all but abandoned. Troops await orders from generals who have started new wars near the capital, and are uncoordinated against the [[Chalican|chalican]] retaliation. Now, front-line soldiers and civilian settlers alike make haste to flee to safer lands.
