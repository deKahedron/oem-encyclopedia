Fomorian's Crusade is an ancient [[Giant|giant]] myth from the late [[Age of Titans]]. It documents the rise and fall of their hero-king [[Fomorian]]. The most common version of the myth is as follows:
### Myth
Lo, [[Toreneas|Queen Toreneas]], last and mightiest ruler of the *uvarjotunen*!

Lo, a realm of palaces upon clouds and fortresses in the liquid mantle of the earth!

Lo, a race at the edge of their of doom.

Lo, [[Giant#Ostoria|Ostoria]]. And this, its downfall.

The giants and the dragons had become the great opposing forces of the world in this Age which the little people we call the Age of the Titans. Into this world of strife, Fomorian was born, the son of a queen and a [[Divine Magic#Gods|god]].
He was as powerful and as beautiful as the dawn itself, but also brilliant of mind and tongue. His people loved him as his enemies feared him.

Even amongst his foes, Fomorian was admired. Befriended he the prince of the serpentine dragons, who flew by his side ever after. The jealous dragons shunned the serpentines for this, branding them ‘*[[Wyvern|wyverns]]*’, a [[Draconic|draconic]] word meaning ‘traitors’.
Everyone believed that Fomorian would become the greatest hero of his people, a conquering warrior who would attain a divine position at the right hand of his godly father [[Annam]].

Encouraged by the faith of his subjects and by a series of victories over the wyrms, Fomorian decided that his quest would not be complete until he had won the approval of the All-Father, in whose name he would conquer all the planes.

He travelled first to [[Avernus]], the first of the [[The Nine Hells|Nine Hells]]. There, he defeated, overthrew and banished the ruling archduke. He tore down his statues and uprooted his dungeons and ordered his friend and squire, [[Sindri Surtrsen]]—who worked stone as were it fine wool—to build in their place a monument worthy of the gods of the [[Giant#The Ordning *(El Estamento)*|Ordning]]. Sindri erected magnificent columns, so tall that had that dreadful place been graced a sky, it could have rested upon them.
But, spake Fomorian, ‘This place is hot and the air is hard to breathe. It will not be a fitting gift for mine divine father.’

So Fomorian rallied his armies and marched upon [[Ysgard]]. In this place, the spirits of fallen warriors fought in perpetual glory, all of them joyfully hurling themselves towards their impending deaths. Fomorian stood his ground against them for a time, but his respect for these spirits soon soured to disdain. Spake he, ‘These shadows crave only death and glory, with no ambition of legacy bequeathed. In mine sire's kingdom, all sacrifice shall be with purpose, until there remains no absence for which sacrifices must be made.’
Turned he then toward [[Grünwen]], who could not be killed, and ordered her to stand her ground against the warriors of Ysgard until their fury were exhausted and they became liable to reason.

Once again, Fomorian and his host changed course. Descended they upon the [[City of Brass]], on the [[Elemental Planes|Plane of Fire]]. The efreeti who lived there tried to keep him at bay, but they feared Fomorian’s might. Instead, they sent their armies of slaves, the azers. These tiny creatures were unable to stop the Chosen Ones, but their courage and conviction inspired Annam’s son. ‘In my kingdom, there will also be a place for little beings such as these, who fear not the strength of any enemy of their masters.’ And so Fomorian channelled his father’s creative power to carve from hard stone the first [[Dwarf|dwarves]] of the Material Plane.

He then surveyed the vast city he had conquered. It was magnificent; every building and street a work of art worthy of an immortal. But Fomorian was not distracted by these trifles. Remarked he on an important fact: ‘This city is impressive, but it has no room to accommodate my father’s empire’—and true enough, for a short distance from the city walls, the solid ground upon which it stood gave way to an endless sea of flame.
‘My empire shall be eternal in every direction, brimming with life and raw resources to be transformed into monuments. My quest shall not resolve until I find such a place.’

And so Fomorian came across the strange crossroads at the heart of the [[Feywild|Faerie Realm]]. Quickly he noticed that in this place, his courage, his pride and his tenacity were magnified; his very emotions shaped the space around him. Learnt he also that this plane had two queens: the [[Summer Queen|Queen of the Summer Court]] and the [[Winter Queen|Queen of the Winter Court]], ever at odds with one another. Spake he to the denizen fey, ‘Your society is chaotic and disorderly. Set aside your weird ways and join my Empire, where you may live rightly beneath a single hand.’
But the fairies would not yield to Fomorian’s demands. And soon he saw how his mood, already heightened by the influence of that plane, became extreme. His tenacity turned to stubbornness; his pride to arrogance; and his courage to rage. He demanded the surrender of both courts, threatening to lay waste to the entire plane. 
But lacked he in his words the respect one ought show to queens. Put they their quarrel aside – perhaps for the only time – and joined their magic to curse Fomorian. His radiant appearance was reduced to a repulsive, terrifying visage: His mind was wilted, the clairvoyance he had inherited from his mother twisted into a paranoia and madness that would torment him for the rest of his days. Even of his people’s love was he robbed, for no longer could they stand to look him true without falling afoul of similar misfortune.

Fomorian returned to the Material Plane in defeat, a slight upon his father and the entire race of giants. Faced with this, Annam concluded that his creations could never surpass him nor ascend to his side, as ever he had wished. So he abandoned them, exiling himself from this world, never to be seen again.

The Storm Queen shuddered at her son’s torment and the forsaking of her lover. Toreneas then dissolved into a storm, the fiercest and longest-lasting ever to befall the world. Even today, the waters of the [[Bay of Grief]] remain unnavigable.

Fomorian too went into exile, having lost his divine power upon his father's disappearance. Deep into the earth he fled, where none might have to look upon his wretched countenance.

And so the giants were left without gods. The storm giants withdrew from the world, awaiting the day when their father once again would deem them worthy of his favour. Ostoria fell into ruin. Each race of giants carved their own path, the sacred Ordning the sole remnant of their once-magnificent society.
### Variations and discussions
* In some versions of the story, Fomorian also travels to the [[Abyss]], claiming a layer of his own and entrusting it to [[Kostchtchie]].
* A common change involves Fomorian imprisoning [[Tiamat]] in Avernus. Sometimes he is said to have battled Tiamat for control of Avernus, sometimes the battle occurs on the [[Material Plane]].
* Dwarves often contest the fact that they were created in the image of the azers.
