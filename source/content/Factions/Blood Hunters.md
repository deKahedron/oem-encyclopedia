### Practices and symptoms
The Blood Hunters learn to use the blood of the monsters they battle to fuel their own magic. However, when the need is dire, or as a way of further empowering their rites, they draw from their own lifeforce as well.

This drain on their vitality causes all but the most conservative of blood hunters to appear to age far more rapidly than other people. Veteran blood hunters have an unusual look: their skin becomes sallow without wrinkling, their hair turns brittle without greying, their eyes turn bloodshot, sometimes with red pupils and their veins take on dark hues.

The average blood hunter's lifespan is cut short within 10-15 years of their initiation, if their prey doesn't slay them first.
### Origins
The origins of the Blood Hunters are not well documented. They first appeared some time in the early 20th century in the modern-day [[Fuegil & the Manilands|Manilands]].
### Organisation
Each blood hunter guards an area of high danger where specific monsters prowl. A hunter might relocate if a more vulnerable area becomes undefended.

The Blood Hunters have no centralised leadership. Veteran hunters recruit new apprentices from desperate people with nothing to lose, often the victims of the very monsters they hunt.

Blood Hunter initiates are taught the magical techniques of their order alongside the doctrine they adhere to. Initiates remain alongside their mentors for at least a year. Once an initiate is raised to adept status, they relocate to a new territory in need of a guardian.
### Striders
Some blood hunters, known as striders, don't remain put in a single location for long. Instead, they dedicate themselves to pursuing a specific kind of threat, pursuing it wherever it may show itself. These hunters are usually more sociable than the rest of their kind and speak more languages.
