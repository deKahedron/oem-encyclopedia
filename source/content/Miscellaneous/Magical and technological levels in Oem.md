Most of the technology in [[Oem]] roughly aligns with the technological level of Europe and Asia in the 17th century of Earth.
### Availability of civil magic
Most medium or large towns have at least one spellcaster. In smaller villages, hamlets and inns, hedge wizards and spellcaster knights will travel around a certain area, selling their services where they are needed. Most large cities have approximately one professional spellcaster for every 500-1000 people.

Most professional spellcasters do not follow the same progression as typical player characters do. Adventurers learn to cast spells quickly and precisely under extreme conditions. Most court mages may be able to perform comparatively high-level spells, though they might require longer casting times, expensive components or simply have a chance to work incorrectly. Outside of very large cities, it is rare to find a spellcaster able to cast spells above 3rd level.

Many spellcasters inhabit towers located around [[Wellspring|wellsprings]], points of high [[The Laws of Magic#Weave (W)|weave]] where it is easier to cast powerful spells.

Ordinary people who know how to cast a cantrip don't have unlimited mental stamina to cast the cantrip indefinitely. The average [[Elf#High Elf|high elf]], for example, knows how to cast a [[Arcane Magic#Wizards|wizard]] cantrip but lacks the mental energy to cast it more than once or twice a day.
##### Familiars
It is normal for people to be aware of the prevalence of wizards' familiars and [[Druid|druids]]. People seeking to have a private conversation might be very wary of doing so in the presence of beasts.
### Availability of magical items
There were times in history when [[Magic Item|magic items]] were mass-produced, most notably the height of the [[Umaran Empire]], at the hands of the artificer companies of [[Nadiria]]. However, due to the lower quality of many of these items, the number of common items has been in decline since the [[Great Humbling]].

In major cities, it is normal for skilled workers to own a common or uncommon magical item that aids them in their work. Blacksmiths might have an amulet to ward against fire, carpenters might have a small magically-powered sawblade, elite town guards are often equipped with wands of *Web* or *Hold Person*. In most of these cases, the items belong to the workers guilds, and are tracked with great care. City guilds take extreme measures against anyone who steals such a valuable piece of equipment.

Since magic items are so difficult to make without rare [[Magical Materials|magical materials]] or serendipitous miracles, magic item shops are extremely rare. The main places where magic items can be bought are:
* [[Arcaxius]]. The magical university conducts all sorts of [[Arcane Magic|arcane]] experiments, many of which result in powerful magic items which the university researchers sell in order to fund their own further research.
* [[Newhearth]]. The home of the [[Harpers]] is a trading hub for adventurers, who bring with them countless magic items. Adventurers who can't find a use for an item themselves will peddle it for gold or exchange it among their fellows.
### Transportation
##### Aerial transportation
Many monsters have been domesticated for use as flying mounts. Most of these, such as griffons, giant bats, giant bees and [[Wyvernfly|wyvernflies]], are only capable of carrying small creatures. Wyverns are the largest domesticated flying species, but their vicious temperaments mean that they are not ridden by common people.

Other than the [[Gallivespian|gallivespians]], flying mounts are reserved only for the incredibly wealthy or for specialised couriers and envoys.
##### Nautical technology
Due to the variety of extreme dangers in the high seas surrounding Oem, there has been little pressure to develop powerful ocean liners.

The most common types of boats are large sailing ships are used for transporting cargo around the coasts of the continent and smaller river boats. In response to the ubiquity of monsters and spells that produce fire, most sails are designed to be able to be cast off in an emergency.

*Thaumaturgy* and *Message* are commonly used on ships to communicate clearly during storms. It is standard for a ship's captain or first mate to be proficient in the use of at least one of these cantrips.

Specialised spellcasters, called *pilot mages*, are stationed aboard ships to direct the wind, calm the seas and counter hostile spells.
##### Teleportation circles
The spell *Teleportation Circle* has existed since the year 3287. Since then, it has been adopted by most major cities and wizard towers. Teleportation circles are extremely well guarded and usually located far away from sensitive locations.
### Communications
The *Sending* spell was developed in the early 29th century. Since then, it has become the standard means of communicating critical information between cities and keeps.

Most temples will employ a [[Divine Magic#Clerics|clerical]] herald. These clerics use *Thaumaturgy* to spread important news to the commons, filtered through the values and interests of the temple.
### Construction
Magic is rarely used for construction directly because of the comparatively high cost of hiring spellcasters relative to labourers.

Large construction projects will typically hire a mage or two to aid the other workers: levelling and stabilising the ground, building temporary structures, conducting repairs and providing safety through spells like *Feather Fall*.
### Divination
In cities, divination magic is used to predict the weather, incoming monster attacks and other close-to-come threats or boons. However, impersonating as a travelling soothsayer is a common scam that causes people to be more sceptical of such rituals.

The imprecision of future-telling is also well-known. Diviners are usually regarded as an important factor to consider when making political decisions, but not an infallible one.

Some cultures, like the [[Human#Damaran|damaran]] humans or the eastern [[Goliath|goliaths]], place extremely high value on prophecy and long-term divination.
### Entertainment
##### Upper class
For the wealthy and the nobility, hunting is incredibly popular. Beyond common [[Druid#Beast|beasts]], those with means will strive to grow their reputation by hunting great monsters, often in the company of renowned adventurers.

Outdoor sports, board games music are also popular for the upper class, regardless of region. These same people will often pursue some small amount of magical study, both for personal protection and to show off.

At large festivals, hastiludes are still popular. Jousts and mêlées are common in [[Hiamyth]], magical duels in [[Alheuffikh]], wrestling in [[Dûnar]].
##### Lower class
The common people of most of Oem have little time for leisure. Outside of seasonal festivals, they mostly get just one day a week of time off, which is often spend with friends. People will meet at weekly religious functions, travelling theatre plays and magic shows or public houses.

Gambling is one of the few pastimes that transcend class barriers. Both dice and card games are common.

Village festivals are usually filled with delicious food, music and dancing, popular sports and gentrified sports, as well as lots of gambling on those sports events.
### Military technology
##### Armies
Armies on Oem are rarely very large. Due to the prevalence of battlefield magic and deadly areas-of-effect, it is much more common for large forces to split into groups of 20-100 and engage in small skirmishes. Only when storming a city or keep, or when the enemy's magical strength has been exhausted, do very large armies converge into a unified force.

Archers are extremely valued for their ability to threaten spellcasters from far beyond the effective range of almost all destructive spells.

*Sending* and *Animal Messenger* are used to coordinate travelling units.

Infantry are usually supported by specialised units, which vary wildly based on what each culture has available. [[Orc|Orcs]] tame giant bats to provide aerial support; [[Dwarf|dwarves]] use phase spiders and bulettes to infiltrate enemy lines; [[Elf#High Elf|high elves]] have an unusually high amount of spellcasters, and so on.
##### Fortifications
Castles and cities need to be able to defend against flying threats which has caused them to develop differently from our world. In most castles, the main bedchambers will be located close to the ground level, so that they can be evacuated into underground bunkers in an emergency. High towers and tethered air balloons are used to survey the horizon and deploy weapons against flying targets. Alternatively, many cities count on flying units of their own for defence.
##### Weaponry
Firearms are gradually becoming more common across Oem. They were initially mass-produced to deal with the magic-destroying [[Chalican|chalicans]] on the [[The Western Front|Western Front]] of the [[Fuegil & the Manilands|Manilands]], but are now finding uses all across the continent as a powerful, low-training weapon for infantry and town guards.

During the height of the Umaran Empire, the Nadirians developed all sorts of extreme magical weapons, from personal-use [[Copperguns|copperguns]] to weapons of mass destruction, like the [[Psychic Bell]].

The [[Elf#Wood Elf|wood elves]] of the sylvan courts are known for their unusual weaponry, like the gauntlet sword, scissor sword and ring sword. These blades are more notable for their unusual appearance and fighting styles than they are for their effectiveness.
