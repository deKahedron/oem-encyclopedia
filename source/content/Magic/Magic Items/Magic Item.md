A magic item is an object with magical properties. Some items produce a magical effect constantly, others need to be activated by a creature.
### Creation
With few exceptions, magic items cannot be created en masse. Many [[Arcane Magic|arcane]] magic items are created by accident from unexpected spell effects. [[Divine Magic|Divine]] magic items are often the product of a [[Divine Magic#Gods|god's]] direct blessing. [[Druidic Magic|Druidic]] magic items contain a small amount of [[Egregore|egregore]] fungus, which channels a magical power from the druidic mycelial network.

[[Blood Magic|Blood magic]] is the most common origin for magical items: organs or vestiges from magical creatures that are preserved can be used as a power source to emulate the creature's own abilities.

Finally, magic items are sometimes created entirely passively. An item of value that spends a long enough time in a magical or spiritually significant location can become suffused with the ambient power around it.
#### Mass production of magic items
The [[Nadiria|Nadirians]] were known for the mass-production of magic items. In order to source the magical energy needed to imbue so many items without relying on unpredictable phenomena, the Nadirian artificers would draw the magical energy of their own serfs, stored in their [[Arcanimbus|arcanimbi]].
#### Artificer skills
Artificers are experts of magical items, but even they have their limits. An artificer "creates" magical items by temporarily infusing magical items with their own innate power. An artificer, no matter how skilled, cannot make a magical item with permanent effects out of purely mundane materials.

However, artificers can be extremely adept at modifying, combining or transferring the magic from an existing item.
### Attunement
Some items require that a creature attune to them before they can be activated. Attuning to a magic item involved forming a simple connection between the creature's soul and the magic within the item.
### Sapient magic items
There exists a little-understood process by which some wielders of magic items are able to perform a sort of advanced attunement with their item. During this process, a portion of the wielder's soul becomes permanently bound to the item.

The item's effects are greatly enhanced and, when the user dies, the item acquires sapience and takes on some of the character traits of the wielder.

This process exerts a toll on the creature involved. As part of their [[Divine Magic#Heroics|heroic]] power is transferred to the item, the creature's hit points are permanently reduced.
### Complex items
The oldest magic items ever discovered are the [[Arlen Stones]], which date back to before the [[Age of Titans]]. Despite the weakness of the stones’ properties, evidence from the dig site suggests that they were once somewhat more potent.

Like many other historical artefacts, the stones are a credit to the stability of magical items: even after millennia buried underground the magic in the stones is recognisable.

However, these and other examples prove [[Pewell’s law]] of enchantment integrity. The law states that an enchantment is bound to the object’s physical properties, and the greater the deviation from the enchanted form, the weaker and more unstable the enchantment becomes.

In the case of the Arlen Stones, their magic has been lessened by the erosion of their chiselled shapes.

In practice, this information has been known for a long time; every crafter of magic items knows to place the enchantment on the most important and long-lasting single piece of the object: the blade of a sword, an unstrung bow, an unwrapped wand. This way, once the enchantment has been applied, any other parts of the item can be maintained and replaced as needed without hurting the enchantment’s integrity.

Pewell's law shines light the difficulty of enchanting complex items. A modern weapon such as a musket has several important pieces without which it cannot function. Enchanting the entire whole musket is possible but will cause problems as soon as any part of the musket needs replacing, effectively rendering the item as weak as its weakest component.
There are 7 common solutions to this problem, and almost all magic items can be classified based on which of these they follow.
##### Magical material
The item is constructed from a [[Magical Materials|material]] that is inherently magical. Since the magic lies in the material and not the form, the item can be damaged without the magic suffering. However, many such materials still require careful processes to be maintained.
##### Channelled magic
In the same way that a non-magical arcane focus can be used to cast spells using an individual’s own spell slots, some magic items don’t have any true enchantments placed upon them. Instead, their shape, value or significance allows the user or another entity to channel magic through the item. This is sometimes the case with religious relics that conduct the power of a divine being.
##### Enchanted component
A single component of the item is enchanted. This is the most widely applicable option. Because the enchanted component must be distinct from the rest of the item, the magic cannot make use of other parts of the whole item. Many enspelled items are produced this way.
##### Infusion
Artificers are able to channel their own magic into an item. Because infusions are temporary, they have very little risk of degrading; when a piece of the item needs removing or replacing, the infusion can simply be ended and channelled again once the item’s maintenance is complete.
##### Rune composition
Though individual runes represent the minimum quantifiable unit of modern artificery, they can be combined to create far more intense or complex effects. The main limitation for runes is the surface area of the item and the level of detail that the rune carver can work. [[Microscripture]] can be used to further increase the number of runes on a surface. Runes provide a very stable compromise of costs: any rune-carved piece can be replaced by a similarly carved piece.
##### Rune tessellation
Tessellation is a type of rune composition that only uses a single type of rune, repeated in the most surface-efficient pattern. The tessellation patterns for all the elementary runes can be found in [[Claudia Messerholdt|Prof. Claudia Messerholdt’s]] book _Sublime Mosaics: the Second Language of Giants._
##### Single-piece construction
The entire item, or as much of the item as possible, is created as a single piece. This technique is rarely used, as most complex items require many different materials. Even then, the method has two large drawbacks: crafting the item requires more artisanal skill; and the item will likely be less structurally sound or light than a mundane equivalent (there is always a reason why the item would normally be made in multiple parts). Some magical crossbows have been made using this technique, crafted from a single piece of wood or metal.
