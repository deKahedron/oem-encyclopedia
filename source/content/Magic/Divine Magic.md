Divine magic is a type of [[Magic|magic]] that is sourced from an adherence to strong cultural values.
### Gods
A god is a personification of a set of cultural values and practices. Most gods exist within a pantheon, which divides up the total magical power provided by the pantheon's followers among those who most strongly represent those values.

Gods can be split into two main categories: pure gods and incarnate gods.
##### Pure gods
Most gods exist only as a manifestation of a cultural belief. They have no true physical form and occupy no single space within the multiverse, but can sometimes manifest avatars or imbue a paragon with their presence. A pure god cannot be killed, it can only diminish and fade from existence by being side-lined or abandoned by its followers. Gods such as [[Oghma]], [[Bahamut]] or [[Souiek]] are pure gods.
##### Incarnate gods
An incarnate god is a god that has congealed into a physical creature, usually via [[Corraesion|corraesion]]. An incarnate god has much more agency to directly affect the world of their own volition and can exist without any followers or worship. However, they can still gain strength from worship and grant power to their followers. In this way, they are able to exist as a duality of god and mortal.

An incarnate god can be killed. Some examples of Incarnate gods include [[Lolth]], [[Tiamat]] and [[Ta'ux]] (formerly).
### Oaths
A paladin who swears a divine oath doesn't necessarily gain their magic from a specific god or pantheon, but rather from a more universal form of divine magic, sourced from the global belief in strong moral convictions.
### Heroics
Heroics is a little-understood form of divine magic that passively affects certain creatures at all times. It is the magical effect responsible for the increased durability and physical prowess of creatures that are feared, respected or well-known. Because of this phenomenon, powerful heroes, villains and nobles are able to survive being hacked at with swords to a greater extent than ordinary people.
