### The Weave
Rusakov particles, also known as ‘*sraf*’ in [[Sylvan]], are non-elemental particles that exist ubiquitously across all planes of existence and interact to create [[Magic|magic]].
In their latent state, they have no mass and move collectively in indecipherable patterns, seemingly without regard for the laws governing physical particles.
This natural arrangement of the particles, together with their movement, is collectively known as the Weave.
### Rusakov's Law
A spell is the product of the interaction between the Weave and a caster’s intent (a caster is defined here as any creature capable of casting spells). The particles respond to a wide range of mental qualities, from faith and conviction to creativity and cunning. A sufficiently strong and focused feeling can alter the flow of the particles or imbue them with non-zero values of mass or energy.

Some spells are so simple to cast that their impact on the caster is negligible. However, the most difficult ones have devastating effects on casters who are not sufficiently prepared for them; that is, if they are able to perform them at all.

This range of difficulty has been the subject of many studies. As summarised in *Principia Arcana Aplicada* ([[Andrej Rusakov|Prof. Andrej Rusakov]], 3417), this measure of arcane power can be defined by Rusakov’s Law:
P=Sh/(W\*Q)

This law ties together the four values that define an individual instance of a spell. Those properties are:
#### Shift (Sh)
###### *Desplazamiento*
Shift measures the amount of physical change that the spell brings about in the universe. This change can manifest itself in any of the physical properties of matter.

It is important to note that although there is a relationship between matter and energy, the unique behaviour of magic means that these values cannot be added together to produce Shift. If a spell's change was measured in joules, there would be a huge disparity between spells of a similar level that produce mass and those that produce energy.

Instead, Shift is measured in **Muthes (δ)**.
#### Weave (W)
###### *Urdimbre*
Every point in space-time has a particular predisposition towards magic. This property affects the ease with which spells can be cast there, depending on how well the Rusakov particles flow. The vast majority of the multiverse registers between 0.92 and 1.7 **Filams (fil)** on the Hemn scale. This range of values has no appreciable effect on most spells.
#### Quiescence (Q)
###### *Quiescencia*
A spell's Quiescence measures how little suspension of disbelief is required to project the effect upon reality. A spell becomes easier to cast as it becomes more widely-known and standardised. This phenomenon is known as the Tenser effect, and it is responsible, for example, for the comparatively high Shift of *Fireball*, a relatively low-level spell.
There are many ways to increase a spell’s quiescence: 
* A physical manifestation helps the caster to have a point of reference for their magic. For 
example, compare the spells *Levitate* and *Tenser’s Floating Disk*. *Tenser’s Floating Disk*, despite being of a lower level, is capable of lifting the same weight as *Levitate*. This is due, in part, to the fact that the presence of the disc as a physical object makes it easier to accept that the load it carries is flying.
* Material components, especially those with high perceived value, add a sense of equivalent exchange.
* Similarly, a more involved ritual (extending the casting time, or focusing the caster’s full attention on the effect whilst it lasts) helps to sustain the effect more easily.
Quiescence is measured in **Serens (s)**.
#### Power (P)
###### *Potencia*
Magical power, measured in **Thaums (þ)**, represents the total amount of mental and physical effort required to cast a spell. From this property, we derive the spell’s level, which is calculated as log₈(P), rounded to the nearest whole number.

The highest power ever recorded was 1.07 Gþ, a record set by [[Ingritte Skårgard]]; however, it is likely that this feat may have been surpassed by legendary figures such as [[Mordenkainen]], [[Caeran]] or [[Auril]].
